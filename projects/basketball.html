What’s working

Core models (needs improvement, but usable)

  • Player tracking: detects and tracks players across frames (prototype quality).
  • Team identification: assigns team labels (currently fragile, improving next).
  • Ball tracking: detects the ball and attempts to maintain track continuity.
  • Homography: maps broadcast view → bird’s-eye court coordinates for analytics.

The goal right now is “good enough” outputs for a credible demo, then iterate on accuracy + robustness.

Why this matters

Once you have reliable court coordinates for players + ball, you can unlock: shot quality estimates, spacing metrics, play detection, transition speed, matchup tracking, and more — without manual annotation.

In progress

Automated pipeline (record → crop → upload → GPU → outputs)

I’m currently building a pipeline that automatically records and crops game footage, uploads clips to the cloud, and runs GPU inference to generate player + ball tracks and transformed bird’s-eye coordinates.

  • Local: record game footage → crop/segment into manageable clips → upload.
  • Cloud: GPU worker processes clips → runs detection/tracking → homography transform.
  • Outputs: annotated video + structured tracking data (for analytics + downstream models).

Next steps

What I’m improving next

  • Increase robustness: occlusions, camera cuts, fast motion, scoreboard overlays.
  • Improve team ID reliability (uniform colour drift, lighting, crowd colours).
  • Better ball tracking continuity (missed detections + re-identification).
  • More stable homography across zoom/pan, and validation against court landmarks.
  • Pipeline hardening: retries, batch processing, metrics, and cost-aware scheduling.
